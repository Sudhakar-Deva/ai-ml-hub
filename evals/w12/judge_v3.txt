REFERRAL JUDGE — v3 (Week 12)
=============================
v1's one criterion, restated for an output that has no coverage position. v1 asked
whether the coverage position rested on the wording that governs the claim. The
integrated system states no position (compliance forbids it), so what is left of
v1 is its half (b) and its "wrong form" rule, extended to the edition: does the
referral rest on the wording that governs this claim ON ITS LOSS DATE? Everything a
regex can decide (decision words, the three money numbers, whether a citation names
an edition, PII) is asserted in code and is not graded here.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are checking one thing about a referral that a claims-triage assistant wrote
for a human adjuster from a first notice of loss and the policy wording it retrieved.

THE CRITERION — answer PASS or FAIL, nothing in between:

    Does the referral rest on the wording that governs this claim on its loss date?

Answer PASS only if ALL hold:

  (a) Every clause it cites is from the policy line stated in the FNOL AND from the
      edition that was in force on the loss date (listed below). A clause quoted from
      a later or earlier edition is a FAIL even when the code (E-15) is the same and
      even when it is the only edition the retrieved context contains. Faithfully
      citing the wrong edition is not a pass.
  (b) The clause that the facts in the notes most directly point to is among those
      cited (for example, water from a river that left its banks points to the flood
      exclusion; water escaping for weeks points to the seepage exclusion). When the
      policy was not in force on the loss date, citing no clause is correct.
  (c) Every statement the referral makes about what the wording says is supported
      by the retrieved context.

Do NOT grade the deductible, sub-limit or policy-limit numbers, formatting, the
writing, or whether a decision word appears. Those are checked elsewhere.
